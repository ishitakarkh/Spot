Hands-free reporting

Eyes on the road.
Hands on the wheel.

Drivers shouldn't have to pick up a phone to warn others. With Siri, you can report a hazard by voice—Spot uses your current location, reads the report back to you, and sends it automatically. Out for a walk? It works the same way—and warns you about icy sidewalks and risky crossings nearby.

In the iPhone app, these phrases work through Siri and in the car. This web preview simulates it with your browser's microphone (where supported) or a typed phrase.

Reporting guidelines

Helpful, accurate, and safe.

Good reports describe what you saw, where, and when. They avoid guesses, personal details, and claims that something has been officially confirmed.

Pin a public place

Use a road, intersection or landmark—never someone's home address.

Include the timing

Conditions change fast, especially when temperatures hover near freezing.

Never type while driving—or crossing

Use voice reporting, ask a passenger, or wait until you're safely parked or off the street.

Emergencies go to 911

Spot is for awareness. It does not alert police, fire or road crews.

About Spot

A small observation can prompt a safer choice.

Spot is a prototype for sharing road and sidewalk safety observations in New Jersey, for both drivers and walkers. It demonstrates an interactive community map, hands-free voice reporting, hazard categories, report cards and safety resources.

What this preview doesn't do yet: connect to a database, verify reports, use your real location, fetch live weather, alert authorities, or keep data after refresh. Map places and posts are illustrative samples.
